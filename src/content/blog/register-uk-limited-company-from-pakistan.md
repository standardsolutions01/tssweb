The UK remains one of the most popular places for Pakistani founders to set up a company. A UK limited company can help you contract with UK and European clients, access international payment platforms and present a familiar structure to customers and investors.

The good news is that you do not need to live in the UK, hold a UK visa or travel to London to form one. The process is mostly online. The less good news is that the rules have tightened in recent years, particularly around identity verification and registered addresses, so it pays to prepare properly.

This guide walks through the process step by step. It is general information, not legal or tax advice — rules and fees change, so always check the current position on <a href="https://www.gov.uk" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">GOV.UK</a> before you apply.

## Who can form a UK limited company?

A UK private company limited by shares needs:

- **At least one director** — an individual aged 16 or over. There is no UK residency or nationality requirement.
- **At least one shareholder** — this can be the same person as the director.
- **A registered office address in the UK** — in the same part of the UK where the company is registered (England and Wales, Scotland or Northern Ireland).
- **A registered email address** — Companies House uses this to contact the company. It is not shown on the public register.
- **People with significant control (PSCs)** — anyone who owns more than 25% of the shares or voting rights, or otherwise controls the company, must be recorded.

Directors and PSCs must also complete **identity verification** with Companies House. This is a relatively recent requirement, introduced as part of reforms to improve the accuracy of the public register.

## Step 1: Choose and check your company name

Your company name must be unique and must not be "the same as" an existing company's name. Search the Companies House register to check availability.

A few practical points:

- The name must end in "Limited" or "Ltd".
- Some words — such as "Bank", "Royal", "Group" or "International" in certain contexts — are sensitive and may need extra justification or approval.
- Avoid names that could infringe someone else's trade mark. A Companies House approval does not protect you from a trade mark claim.

## Step 2: Decide your structure

Before filing, decide:

- **Who will be the directors?** Directors run the company and carry legal duties.
- **Who will own the shares, and in what proportion?** Many solo founders issue 100 shares of £1 each, but the right structure depends on your plans.
- **Who are the PSCs?** If you hold more than 25%, you are a PSC.
- **What does the company do?** You will need one or more SIC codes describing the business activity.

Most small companies adopt the standard **model articles of association**, which set out how the company is run. Founders with co-founders or investors may want bespoke articles or a shareholders' agreement, and should speak to a solicitor about those.

## Step 3: Arrange a UK registered office address

The registered office is the company's official address. Statutory letters, including those from HMRC and Companies House, are sent there, and it appears on the public register.

Since 2024, the registered office must be an "appropriate address" — somewhere that documents will come to the attention of someone acting on the company's behalf. A PO box alone is not acceptable.

If you do not have a UK address, you can use a registered office service. Choose a reputable provider that will scan and forward your post, because missing a letter from HMRC or Companies House can lead to penalties.

## Step 4: Complete identity verification

Every director and PSC must verify their identity with Companies House. There are two main routes:

1. **Directly with GOV.UK**, using GOV.UK One Login and an accepted photo ID.
2. **Through an Authorised Corporate Service Provider (ACSP)** — a formation agent or accountant registered with Companies House to carry out verification.

For founders in Pakistan, the ACSP route is often easier, as the accepted documents and online checks for the direct route can vary. Once verified, each person receives a personal code that is used on filings.

## Step 5: File the incorporation application

With the name, structure, address and verification ready, the incorporation application is submitted to Companies House. It includes:

- The company name and registered office address.
- Details of directors, shareholders and PSCs.
- A statement of capital (the shares being issued).
- The articles of association.
- SIC codes.
- A registered email address.
- A **lawful purpose statement** confirming the company is being formed for a lawful purpose.

The Companies House fee is payable at this stage. Fees have changed several times recently, so **check the current fee on the official GOV.UK website**.

When approved, you receive a **certificate of incorporation** showing your company number. Online applications are often approved within one working day, though this is not guaranteed.

## Step 6: Set up your tax registrations

Incorporation is the beginning, not the end. After registration:

### Corporation Tax

HMRC usually posts a **Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)** to the registered office shortly after incorporation. You must register the company for Corporation Tax within three months of starting any business activity, such as buying, selling, advertising or employing people.

### VAT

VAT registration becomes compulsory once taxable turnover passes the VAT threshold. You can also register voluntarily, which some businesses selling to other VAT-registered businesses choose to do. Check the current threshold on GOV.UK.

### PAYE

If the company will pay salaries — including to you as a director — it may need to register as an employer for PAYE.

## Step 7: Open a business bank account

This is often the most challenging step for non-resident founders. Traditional UK high-street banks often expect directors to live in the UK. Several electronic money institutions and digital business accounts do accept non-resident directors, though their criteria change and approval is always at their discretion.

Prepare in advance:

- Certificate of incorporation and company number.
- Passport and proof of address for directors and significant shareholders.
- A short description of the business, expected customers and transaction volumes.
- A website or online presence, where possible.

Clear, consistent information makes approval far more likely.

## Step 8: Stay compliant every year

A UK company has ongoing obligations, even if it is not trading much:

| Obligation | When |
|---|---|
| Confirmation statement to Companies House | At least once every 12 months |
| Annual accounts to Companies House | First accounts: usually 21 months after incorporation; then 9 months after the year end |
| Company Tax Return (CT600) to HMRC | 12 months after the end of the accounting period |
| Corporation Tax payment | Usually 9 months and 1 day after the end of the accounting period |
| VAT returns (if registered) | Usually quarterly |

Late filing leads to automatic penalties, and repeated failures can lead to the company being struck off. The simplest protection is to keep your books up to date from day one. Our [bookkeeping and accounting service](/bookkeeping-accounting/) keeps records ready for every deadline.

## Points Pakistani founders should consider

- **Tax residence:** A UK company that is managed and controlled from Pakistan may have tax consequences in Pakistan as well as the UK. The UK–Pakistan double taxation agreement may affect the outcome. Take professional tax advice before trading.
- **Foreign exchange rules:** Pakistani residents investing or holding assets abroad should check the current State Bank of Pakistan foreign exchange regulations and FBR declaration requirements.
- **Records:** Keep copies of all incorporation documents, share certificates and statutory registers in one place.
